Methods

How beta(t) is estimated, validated, and turned into cfs

1. The response comes from the null IPM

Recruitment is log(recruits / spawner), taken from the posterior of the flow-naive integrated population model, with a recruit lag from spawning to the age at which fish are counted.

That “flow-naive” is not a detail. Using the flow-informed IPM’s abundances as the response would put flow on both sides of the regression: the response would already contain the flow effect the regression is trying to estimate, and the result would be biased toward finding one. Survival is modelled on the logit scale, because a multiplier on a probability is meaningless at 0.95 and trivial at 0.05.

Two lags are easy to confuse and they mean different things. The recruit lag is biology: fish counted at age 2-3 were spawned three years earlier, so they pair with that year’s stock. The flow lag is statistics: which calendar year’s hydrograph the IPM’s own model selection chose. When the flow lag equals the recruit lag, the mechanism sits in the spawning year - adult access, redd site selection, incubation. One less, and it sits in the age-0 rearing year - emergence timing, displacement, first-summer rearing. Different hypotheses, and a wrong value shifts everything by a year while still producing a plausible curve.

2. Standardizing the hydrograph

Three steps, in this order, and the order matters.

  1. Logs. Discharge is multiplicative; 50 cfs means something different in April and September.
  2. Subtract the day’s mean across years. Now a positive value means “wetter than this date usually is”, not “it is June”. Without this, beta(t) spends itself fighting the seasonal cycle, which dwarfs any anomaly.
  3. Divide by one global standard deviation - not by each day’s own. Per-day standardization would make a 2% wobble in a stable January week and a doubling in June both equal “one SD”, erasing exactly the difference beta(t) is meant to weigh.

The geometric-mean discharge on each day, Qbar(t), is kept, because it is the bridge from beta(t) to cfs (section 5).

3. Estimating beta(t)

365 unknown coefficients from roughly 40 years cannot be fitted directly. The hydrographs themselves supply the constraint: they are smooth and highly autocorrelated, so a handful of modes of variation describe nearly all of how years differ. beta(t) is estimated inside the span of those modes and rotated back onto the day axis.

The objection, stated plainly. The modes are ranked by variance in the predictor, without reference to the response. A low-variance flow mode that carries the real biological signal is discarded before it is ever tested - the standard critique of principal-component regression (Jolliffe 1982, Applied Statistics 31:300-303). beta(t) cannot show structure the retained modes cannot build. Changing the retention threshold swaps one arbitrary number for another; it does not answer the objection, and it belongs in the paper rather than a footnote.

4. The gate

beta(t) always comes out of the arithmetic. The gate decides whether it means anything, and it is the most important output in the project.

r2_insample is optimistic by construction: the model has seen these years and spent edf degrees of freedom on about forty of them. Context only.

r2_loo versus r2_blocked. Leave-one-out leaves a held-out year’s immediate neighbours in the training set, and both hydrographs and responses are autocorrelated between adjacent years, so the model can half-recognize the test year from its neighbours. The gap between the two is that leak, made visible. Blocked cross-validation holds out contiguous runs of years instead, and can be negative - meaning worse than predicting the long-run mean, which is the honest verdict when a model has learned noise. That is why the threshold is “greater than zero” rather than something comfortable.

p_value comes from permuting the alignment between response and flow while preserving each series’ own autocorrelation. The null is not “flow is noise”; it is “this response, paired with the wrong years of this flow record”. Its smallest attainable value is finest_p. A p equal to that floor means no permutation matched the true alignment, not that p is precisely that number.

Both conditions must hold. Where two estimator arms were run, agreement between them is a further requirement: if they disagree, the daily structure is coming from the estimator rather than the data, and no daily recommendation is defensible.

5. From beta(t) to cfs

beta(t) is per standard deviation of log anomaly. A manager releases cfs. Adding dQ on day t changes the standardized anomaly by log(1 + dQ/Q)/sigma, so at that day’s baseline discharge the benefit per cfs is approximately

\[ \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial Q}(t) \;\approx\; \frac{\beta(t)}{\sigma\,\bar{Q}(t)} \]

Because discharge spans more than an order of magnitude across the year, dividing by Qbar(t) reshapes the curve. The best day to release water is often not the day with the largest beta(t), and half the management answer lives in that division.

6. The two Reclamation questions

One year at a time (script 08). Each scenario-year’s hydrograph is scored with beta(t), standardized against the training climatology - never against the scenario’s own, which would re-centre a uniformly drier future to look average and make the effect vanish. The day-by-day attribution then separates two cases a single number cannot: flow dropping on days that matter, and flow dropping a lot on days that do not.

Sustained, at equilibrium (script 10). The same forecasts held indefinitely, propagated through the population model to equilibrium abundance and maximum surplus production. It can point the other way from the annual view, because equilibrium compounds.

The current exports build each future period by shifting the 1950-1999 sequence forward by 45, 75 and 105 years. Every 29 February then lands on 28 February of a non-leap year and collides with it, and the reverse case empties 29 February in the target leap years. Both are handled explicitly. A consequence for interpretation: all three periods share one historical weather sequence, so the across-year spread within a scenario is that record’s own variability and the period contrasts are paired by construction.

7. What this does not establish

The gate tests prediction, not causation. A validated beta(t) predicts years the model has not seen; it does not identify the mechanism, say whether the relation holds outside the observed flow range, or confirm that the retained modes were the right ones. Flow recommendations ultimately rest on mechanism, and this analysis constrains it rather than supplying it.
