Methods

How the weighted hydrographs are estimated, averaged, validated and pooled

1. The response comes from the null IPM

Recruitment is log(recruits / spawners), taken from the posterior of the flow-naive integrated population model, with a recruit lag from spawning to the age at which fish are counted.

That “flow-naive” is not a detail. Using the flow-informed IPM’s abundances as the response would put flow on both sides of the regression: the response would already contain the flow effect the regression is trying to estimate, and the result would be biased toward finding one. Every posterior draw is carried through the regressions, not just the posterior mean, so IPM uncertainty is in every interval on this site.

2. Three hydrograph years per cohort

A cohort counted as recruits lived through several hydrographs before it was counted, and each touches a different part of its life. Every site is fitted at each of them:

Life stages, as set by stage_lags in config.R.
stage years before recruitment what flow could act on
spawning year 3 adult access, redd-site choice, incubation and emergence (into the next spring)
age-0 year 2 emergence timing, first-summer rearing, displacement, first winter
age-1 year 1 second summer and winter; habitat shifts with size

The IPM selected one flow lag per site on a summer covariate. Here it is only a label (marked * in the tables): every stage is tested on the whole hydrograph, and the Among sites page asks whether the IPM’s choice and the daily hydrograph agree.

On the figures the three years are laid end to end on a 1095-day life-cycle axis, so incubation (December to March) reads as one stretch rather than two halves in different calendar years. Fitted one stage at a time (sections 4-5), neighbouring stages partly echo each other when wet years follow wet years; the pooled models (section 8) fit the stages jointly, which removes that echo.

3. Standardizing the hydrograph

Three steps, in this order, and the order matters.

  1. Logs. Discharge is multiplicative; 50 cfs means something different in April and September.
  2. Subtract the day’s mean across years. Now a positive value means “wetter than this date usually is”, not “it is June”. Without this, beta(t) spends itself fighting the seasonal cycle, which dwarfs any anomaly.
  3. Divide by one global standard deviation – not by each day’s own. Per-day standardization would make a 2% wobble in a stable January week and a doubling in June both equal “one SD”, erasing exactly the difference beta(t) is meant to weigh.

4. Each river’s flow modes

365 unknown daily coefficients from a few decades of recruitment cannot be fitted directly. The hydrographs supply the constraint: they are smooth and highly autocorrelated, so a handful of modes of variation (functional principal components) describe nearly all of how one year’s hydrograph differs from another’s. beta(t) is estimated inside the span of those modes and rotated back onto the day axis.

The modes come from the river’s whole flow record, not just the years with fish data, and they are found before any fish data are used. Each mode’s sign is fixed so that a positive score means a wetter-than-usual year over most of the mode.

How many modes. Modes are kept until they carry 90% of the flow variation, and each kept mode must carry at least the share of one 2-year block of the record: with n flow years, a floor of 100 x 2 / n percent. A mode weaker than that is a pattern a couple of unusual years could produce on their own. At most 20 modes.

The objection, stated plainly. The modes are ranked by variance in the predictor, without reference to the response. A low-variance flow mode that carries the real biological signal is discarded before it is ever tested – the standard critique of principal-component regression (Jolliffe 1982, Applied Statistics 31:300-303). beta(t) cannot show structure the retained modes cannot build.

The floor makes this sharper, not softer, and in a predictable place. On a regulated river the autumn hydrograph varies little from year to year, so the mode that carries autumn variation is small and is the first one the floor removes – and autumn is when brown trout spawn. A flat spawning-season curve at a tailwater is therefore exactly what the estimator would produce whether or not autumn flow matters there. Two checks run alongside: a penalized-spline estimator that does not use the modes (section 6), and the season-by-stage anchor, which does not either. Where they show an autumn effect the mode curve does not, believe them.

5. Which modes: model averaging

Kept modes are not all used at full size. For each site and stage, every subset of up to 5 modes is fitted by least squares, with spawner abundance (standardized) in every model, and ranked by AICc (MuMIn::dredge). Models within 4 AICc of the best are kept, their Akaike weights renormalized, and each mode’s coefficient averaged over them, counting as zero in models that leave it out (the “full” average). A mode the data do not support is shrunk toward zero rather than kept at full size or dropped outright, which is a gentler and more honest version of choosing modes by hand.

A mode’s importance w is the summed weight of the kept models that contain it. w near 1 means every plausible model uses it; it is not a p-value, and a mode can have w = 1 and a small effect.

Uncertainty. Each coefficient’s variance is the sum of three parts: the weighted within-model variance, the spread of the coefficient between the kept models, and the spread across IPM posterior draws. All draws are fitted at once, B = (X'X)^-1 X'Y, so draws cost nothing extra. The model set is chosen on the posterior-mean response and held fixed across draws.

beta(t) for a stage is then the weighted sum of mode shapes: beta(t) = sum_k b_k phi_k(t). The site pages draw each mode’s piece of it separately, so a feature of the curve can be traced to the mode that makes it.

A stage passes when both hold:

  1. cv_gain > 0. The blocked cross-validated R-squared of the flow model minus that of spawner abundance alone: what flow adds, out of sample, beyond density dependence. Folds hold out runs of 3 consecutive years, because hydrographs and responses are both autocorrelated and leave-one-out would let a model half-recognize a test year from its neighbours. The whole model-averaging step is redone inside every fold – choosing modes on all the data and then cross-validating would leak the test years into the choice.
  2. p_adj <= 0.1. The permutation null is “this response, paired with the wrong years of this flow record”: the response is rotated against the flow series (every circular shift, and every shift of the time-reversed series), which preserves each series’ own autocorrelation. The statistic is the in-sample R-squared of the model with all kept modes (not re-averaged per alignment; the null hypothesis is the same and re-dredging 2n - 1 times would multiply the run time). The p-value is Holm-adjusted across the three stages, because three stages were tried. p_floor is the smallest adjusted value attainable with that many years; where it exceeds 0.1, no stage at that site can pass by construction, and the page says so.

cor_fpc_penalized is not part of the pass rule but is reported beside it. A second estimator – a penalized spline in day of year, smoothness chosen by REML, no modes – is fitted to the same data. Where its curve and the mode curve disagree in shape, the daily structure is coming from the estimator rather than the data, and only seasonal statements are defensible.

Adult survival (script 04) is gated the same way, on the logit-scale process deviate from the null IPM, against the same year’s hydrograph or the year before; its p-value is multiplied by the number of lags tried.

7. One set of flow modes for every river

Each river’s own modes are the right description of that river, but they are different shapes at different sites, so their coefficients cannot be compared or pooled. Script 06 runs one functional PCA over every river’s standardized hydrographs (sites that read the same gage enter once) and keeps 90% of the variation, at most 10 modes. Every site-year is then scored on these common modes.

8. Pooling: the Montana-wide picture

One row per site and recruit year. Every site keeps its own intercept and its own density dependence (site + site:stock); only the flow terms are shared, and all three stages enter together, so each stage’s effect is estimated holding the others fixed. Fitted in mgcv by REML.

The pooled models (R/07_pooled_lifecycle.R).
model flow terms role
A common modes x stage, a Montana-wide slope plus a random slope per site primary
A0 common modes x stage, Montana-wide slope only tests whether sites differ (A vs A0)
B one penalized spline over the whole 1095-day life cycle (k = 40) robustness: different assumptions, no modes
C four seasons x three stages, Montana-wide slope plus a random slope per site pre-registered, low-resolution anchor

Partial pooling. In A and C each site’s slope is the Montana-wide slope plus a site departure that is shrunk toward zero by as much as that site’s data are uncertain; the size of the departures (a variance component per term) is itself the answer to “do populations respond alike?”.

IPM uncertainty. With smoothing parameters and variance components held at their REML values, every coefficient is a linear function of the response, so all 10^{4} posterior draws at every site go through one matrix product (checked exact against refitting to 1e-15). Intervals add between-draw and within-fit covariance, as in section 5.

Cross-validation. Folds hold out the same block of calendar years at every site at once, so a shared wet or dry year cannot be in training at one river and in test at another. R-squared is computed on within-site variation, so differences in site means do not inflate it, and reported as a gain over site + stock alone.

How populations differ. The site departures (BLUPs) from model A are put through a PCA of their own: populations placed close together respond to flow alike, whatever their mean recruitment.

9. What this does not establish

  • The gate tests prediction, not causation. A validated curve predicts years the model has not seen; it does not identify the mechanism, or say whether the relation holds outside the observed flow range.
  • Life-history windows are provisional. The shaded spawning, incubation, emergence and rearing windows on the figures are placeholders until replaced with cited, river-specific dates.
  • Pooling assumes the common modes describe each river adequately. Where section 7 shows they do not, that river’s contribution to the Montana-wide curve is through a poor description of its flow.
  • A few dozen years against 365 days. Seasonal statements are usually supportable; single days often are not.
